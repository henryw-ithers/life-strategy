# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

Henry first: the app is designed around the builder's own daily use —
a reflective person who wants strategy behind their checklist, opening
the app briefly each morning and evening, and sitting down once a
month for a longer review. Built to share: every decision should hold
up for a future public release to people who liked Strack's HBR life
strategy piece, journaling apps, or habit trackers but wanted the
"why" layer those tools lack. When real use and hypothetical users
conflict, real use wins.

The app ships one custom design language on both iOS and Android — it
should feel like itself, not like a system app on either platform.

## Product Purpose

A personal planning and reflection app that turns a monthly life
diagnostic — importance × satisfaction ratings across 16 strategic
life units — into a derived daily checklist and a grade out of 100.
Daily execution is a checklist and a number; monthly reflection is a
ritual (diagnostic, portfolio graph, goal review); the long-term
record reads as a log of a life, with journals, photos, and flagged
memories alongside grades.

Success, ultimately: the daily grade converges with the user's own
felt contentment (the calibration experiment). Success for v1: Henry
uses it every day without friction or resentment.

## Positioning

Habit apps start with tasks; this starts with what matters. The
strategy layer derives the checklist. One line: align daily action
with what matters most.

## Brand Personality

Clear, confident, encouraging. A trusted coach with a clipboard —
crisp hierarchy, decisive color, satisfying check-off moments. Warmth
arrives through celebration (completed goals, special days, look-back
memories), not through softness or decoration. Copy is direct and
kind; it never nags, never guilt-trips, and frames every score as a
guideline.

## Anti-references

- Project-management software: Jira/Notion-style density, nested
  configuration, dashboard sprawl. The daily surface is a checklist,
  not a workspace.
- Streak-shame gamification: Habitica-style punishment mechanics,
  Duolingo-style nagging mascots, alarm colors on missed days,
  loss-aversion tricks.
- SaaS dashboard aesthetics: hero metrics with gradient accents,
  identical stat-card grids.
- Social-comparison surfaces: leaderboards, shared scores,
  competitive anything.

## Design Principles

1. **Strategy before execution.** The diagnostic is the front door;
   every task exists because the user said something matters.
2. **Daily simplicity.** The daily surface is a checklist and a
   number — openable, actionable, and closable in under a minute.
3. **Gentle by design.** Low grades get neutral, kind presentation;
   scores are guidelines and the app says so; no shame mechanics
   anywhere.
4. **A tool, not a taskmaster.** Partial use is valid use; returning
   after absence never earns a guilt trip.
5. **The log is a record of a life.** Over time, memories (journals,
   photos, special days, achievements) outrank metrics in what the
   app resurfaces.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast throughout; full dynamic-type / font-scaling
support; reduced-motion alternatives for the portfolio graph
animations and celebrations; screen-reader labels on all interactive
elements, including the custom Skia charts (data available as
accessible text, not only pixels).
